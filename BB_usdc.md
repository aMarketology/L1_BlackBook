To facilitate a transaction from USDC to your custom BB token using OneKey on your custom L1, you essentially need to bridge the "Solana ecosystem" with your "Embedded SVM ecosystem."Because you are using a 2-of-3 SSS (Shamir Secret Sharing) model where OneKey acts as one of the shards, the transaction isn't just a simple "send." It is a multi-stage assembly.Here is the theoretical stack you need to facilitate this:1. The Gateway (The "Bridge")Since USDC lives on Ethereum or Solana, but your BB token lives on your custom L1, you need a Tier 1 Gateway.The Asset: You need a "Wrapped USDC" (wUSDC) mint on your L1.The Bridge Program: An Anchor program that "locks" native USDC on Solana and "mints" wUSDC on your L1 (or vice versa).Liquidity: A pool on your L1 (USDC/BB) so the user has something to swap against.2. The OneKey Connection (RPC Compatibility)For OneKey to "see" your chain, your L1 must provide a Solana-compatible JSON-RPC.Custom RPC URL: The user adds your node URL (e.g., https://rpc.bb-chain.io) to OneKey.IDL Compatibility: OneKey needs to understand your Swap instruction. If you use standard Anchor, you can host the IDL (Interface Definition Language) so the wallet can show the user: "Swap 10 USDC for 500 BB" instead of a "blind signature."3. The Hybrid Signer Assembly (The 2-of-3 Logic)This is where your specific architecture comes in. OneKey doesn't know it's "part" of a 2-of-3 SSS; it thinks it's signing a standard Solana transaction.The "Signer Service": You need a middleman service (often called a Relayer or Co-signer).The Flow:Frontend: Prepares a transaction to your swap_usdc_for_bb Anchor program.Shard 1 (OneKey): The user clicks "Confirm" on their OneKey. This provides the first signature.Shard 2 (Your Server): Your backend verifies the user is authorized (e.g., MFA/Session) and provides the second signature using its internal shard.Assembly: Your node "assembles" these into a valid transaction that satisfies the 2nd-of-3 requirement.4. The Execution (The SVM Runtime)Once the transaction hits your L1:AccountsDB: The SVM checks if the user has enough USDC in their "Account" (the $u64$ balance from your plan).BPF Execution: Your Anchor program runs the swap math (likely a simple Constant Product formula: $x * y = k$).Mint/Burn: The program burns (or transfers) the USDC and mints (or transfers) the BB tokens to the user’s wallet address.In Summary: What you need to buildOn-Chain: An Anchor Swap Program and a USDC Mint.Off-Chain: A "Co-signing Service" that holds the second SSS shard.Interface: A frontend that talks to OneKey via the standard @solana/web3.js library, but redirects the signed transaction to your Co-signing service instead of a public RPC.Which part of this flow would you like to drill into first?
